# Source Policy

Use the strongest available source and mark weak evidence clearly. Tier 3 sources cannot support a thesis alone.

## Tier 1 Sources

- SEC filings, including 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, S-1, S-3, 424B, DEF 14A, and prospectus supplements.
- Official company investor relations releases and presentations.
- Exchange filings and official listing notices.
- FDA pages and official government agency sources.
- Audited annual financial statements.

Tier 1 sources should anchor financial facts, dilution history, cash runway analysis, risk factors, and filing dates.

## Tier 2 Sources

- Reputable financial news organizations.
- Established financial data providers.
- Earnings call transcript providers.
- Industry reports with clear publisher identity and methodology.

Tier 2 sources can provide context, but material claims should be checked against Tier 1 sources when possible.

## Tier 3 Sources

- Social media.
- Forums and message boards.
- Blogs.
- Influencer posts.
- Promotional newsletters.
- Unsourced screenshots.

Tier 3 sources may suggest questions, but they cannot support a thesis alone. If a thesis depends on Tier 3 evidence, stop and seek SEC or official source support.

## Evidence Rules

- Keep facts, calculations, estimates, and opinions separate.
- Preserve `MISSING` when a financial field is unknown.
- Link or name the filing and filing date used.
- Prefer recent filings over stale data.
- Do not use paid APIs unless explicitly reviewed and approved in a future phase.
