# Source Policy

This lab prioritizes primary sources and reliable financial data. Every research memo should clearly separate confirmed facts from estimates, interpretations, and unverified claims.

## Strong Sources

- SEC filings, including 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, S-1, S-3, S-8, DEF 14A, and prospectus supplements.
- Company investor relations releases.
- Exchange filings and official listing notices.
- Official earnings call transcripts.
- Official regulatory updates, such as FDA communications when publicly available.
- Official government sources, including SEC, FDA, CMS, DOE, EPA, and relevant court or agency pages.
- Exchange notices and official listing information.
- Audited annual financial statements.

## Useful Secondary Sources

- Established financial data providers.
- Reputable financial news organizations.
- Analyst notes only when used as opinion, not as primary evidence.
- Industry reports, when the publisher and methodology are clear.

## Weak Sources

- Social media posts.
- Message boards.
- Forums.
- Blogs.
- Influencer posts.
- Promotional newsletters.
- Unsourced screenshots.
- Rumors about customers, financing, FDA events, or acquisition interest.

Weak sources may suggest questions, but they should not drive a trade decision unless confirmed by stronger sources.

## Citation Expectations

- Link or name the filing and date used.
- Record the period covered by each financial statement.
- Note whether a number is reported, calculated, or estimated.
- Flag stale data when newer filings may be available.
