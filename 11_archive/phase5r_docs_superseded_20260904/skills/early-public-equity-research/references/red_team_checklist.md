# Red-Team Checklist

Use this checklist before any paper-trade planning. A red-team note is required before a company can move beyond research review.

## Business Model Risk

- What does the company sell?
- Who pays, and why?
- Is the product essential or discretionary?
- Is revenue recurring, transactional, project-based, or milestone-based?

## Liquidity Risk

- Is dollar volume sufficient for entry and exit?
- Is the spread likely to be costly?
- Is the float small or tightly held?
- Are lockups, registration statements, or insider sales relevant?

## Dilution Risk

- Are there recent offerings, ATM programs, warrants, converts, S-3 shelves, S-8 registrations, or prospectus supplements?
- Does the company need cash soon?
- Would dilution be likely before the thesis has time to play out?

## Cash Runway Risk

- How much cash and equivalents are reported?
- What is the quarterly burn?
- How many quarters of runway remain?
- Is management relying on financing, customer prepayments, grants, partnerships, or asset sales?

## Valuation Risk

- What has to go right to justify the current valuation?
- Is the valuation supported by revenue, margins, cash flow, assets, or optionality?
- What multiple compression or downside scenario is plausible?

## Event Risk

- Are earnings, FDA decisions, clinical data, financings, lockup expirations, customer announcements, regulatory decisions, or litigation dates approaching?
- Could an event invalidate the thesis quickly?

## Sector-Specific Risk

- Biotech: clinical trial failure, FDA delay, endpoint risk, financing risk.
- Clean tech: project financing, commodity costs, subsidies, tariffs, supply chain.
- Semiconductors: cycle risk, customer concentration, capex timing, export controls.
- Software infrastructure: retention, net expansion, cloud cost, competition, security events.
- AI infrastructure: capex cycle, supply constraints, customer concentration, depreciation, model/platform shifts.

## Thesis Invalidation

- What fact would prove the thesis wrong?
- What filing item should be checked first?
- What price/risk condition would invalidate a paper-trade plan?
- Is the evidence strong enough without social media, forums, blogs, or influencer posts?
